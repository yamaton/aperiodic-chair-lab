## Re-derived arguments

- **Cubic rigidity (`PORT_SIMPLIFICATION.md:116-169`).** The monic defining polynomial z-H psi divides a pulled-back coincident degree-three defining polynomial; equal total degrees force a constant quotient. A surface line projects nontrivially. Its cubic coefficient forces d=0, e=0, or d+e=0; the quadratic coefficients then force precisely x=0, y=0, or x+y=1. All three lines lie in z=0. Their three intersections form a triangle with side lengths 1,2,sqrt(5); its metric stabilizer is trivial. The base plane, ordered tangent axes, anchor and absolute depth therefore follow from an arbitrary surface-open coincidence. Normal reversal is the only residual choice. The divisibility argument does not presume a lattice or a finite orientation list.
- **Width supremum (`PORT_DIMENSIONS.md:86-140`).** A convex support straddling a reflection axis shares an interior point on that axis with its reflected support. Thus a disjoint D4 orbit lies in reflection wedges. In the normalized wedge 0<=y<=x<=1/2 the three displayed linear inequalities eliminate A,B exactly, giving six bounds 1/6 and two bounds 1/4. The strict family a=-1/4, b=1/8+w/2 has y>=epsilon, x+y<=-epsilon and x>=-1/4-w with epsilon=1/8-w/2. Hence all widths below 1/4 are attainable with strict separation. For a seven-low face, a reflection-overlap pair and its half-turn image involve four distinct frame indices: in the regular D4 frame action the half turn is neither the identity nor the reflection. Removing one slot cannot remove both pairs. This validates the stated extension to two depth-dependent offsets with common w; it does not prove unrestricted packing optimality.
- **Depth envelope (`PORT_DIMENSIONS.md:144-225`).** Same-face zones are separated by support separation; parallel planes are at least 1 apart and 2h<1. For perpendicular grid planes, a point in both zones would have distances s_A,s_B satisfying |s_A|<d_A<=|s_B| and the reverse, impossible. The completed-square identity for P(u)-psi is correct. Differentiating (C+wu)/P yields the quadratic 2wu^2+(w+3C)u+w+C; the stated C=1/4 formula follows. At w approaching 1/4, cancellation gives 8/[27(1-u)^2], whose infimum is 2/27. The four Bernstein intervals certify d-h psi>=5/256 for the concrete h=1/8 witness, uniformly rather than by samples.
- **Depth-dependent witness (`PORT_DIMENSIONS.md:67-76`).** Shifting only high ports by U/128 preserves frame translations because matching magnitudes share offsets. In the witness's reference wedge, -x remains the dominant absolute tangent coordinate after the shift, so nearest-edge distance indeed increases by 1/128. My extra probe verified all 112 pairs obtained by assigning either depth to every pair of distinct slots; they remain strictly separated. This is stronger than checking only assignments appearing on the 24 actual faces.
- **Frame and atlas transfer (`PORT_DIMENSIONS.md:48-79,257-285`).** Open-patch rigidity recovers ordered U,V and opposite N, with equal magnitude/opposite signed depth. The common or magnitude-dependent offsets cancel exactly from p_A-Rp_B. Existing fitting interfaces therefore retain their poses. Conversely a matching new interface can only use those same key/frame pairs. Along with separated local modifications this transfers fine and full-macro interface predicates; it does not assert that a macroboundary is a scaled copy of the one-tile boundary.
- **Connectedness and global grid (`PORT_DIMENSIONS.md:230-255`).** The normal modification reach h=1/8 leaves every carrier cube's middle-half core intact. Thickening to H=9/64 retains at least 1/256 clearance and gives disjoint supports for the piecewise-linear normal homeomorphisms; as psi tends to zero their displacements tend to zero, so the seam gluing is continuous. Each cap mate yields the neighboring carrier cube, duplicate ownership contradicts common retained cores, and lattice adjacency propagates ownership. With rho=1/7, h<rho and 3rho^2<1/16. Clamping the center of any putative additional tile's retained radius-1/4 ball into an owned cube's rho-inset produces a point in both interiors. This excludes a second component without assuming that the curved material of the first component already fills space. The arbitrary cap-mate existence step correctly relies on local finiteness from disjoint retained balls, then a finite closed boundary-piece cover.
